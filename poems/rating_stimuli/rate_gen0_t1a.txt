The year turns down its page, a fading gold,
And swallows chart their long and silent way;
The fields, once warm, are now a story told
In stubble gray beneath a lingering day.
The garden’s last, late rose surrenders breath
To winds that carry rumors of the frost;
Each bough lets go its hold, and what was health
Becomes a memory, lovely and soon lost.
Yet I, who watch the quiet world unbind,
Find no despair in what must fall and flee;
The heart, like earth, must leave its bloom behind
To learn the patience of the coming tree.
So let this parting be a gentle art—
I keep the warmth, and let the season start.
