The wind strips gold from branches, one by one,
And bears the tattered leaves on hurried wings;
The amber light of afternoon is done,
And silence settles where the robin sings.
The garden paths are strewn with faded blooms,
The scented air grows thin and sharp with frost;
A lonely swallow traces through the glooms,
And all the summer’s promises are lost.
But O, this parting is not final grief—
It is the earth’s slow breathing, deep and still,
A turning inward, root and sleeping leaf,
A quiet trust that answers winter’s will.
So let the cold wind take what it must take—
The seed remembers what the season makes.
