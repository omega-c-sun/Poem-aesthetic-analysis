The mind is not a ledger, but a sea
That keeps its ledgers in a liquid sleep,
And every wave a thrice-remembered plea
From coasts the sunken years have ceased to keep.
I cast a stone of thought—and watch it fall
Through fathoms where the drowned days turn and gleam,
Their faces blurred against a coral wall,
The wreck of every unaccomplished dream.
But now the tide turns hard, and salt-stung air
Unravels what the patient deep has spun—
The bright particular grief I used to bear
Comes surfacing, a ghost beneath the sun,
And I must drink that brine, must taste the whole
Cold archive of the water in my soul.
