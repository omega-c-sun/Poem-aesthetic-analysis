The city sheds its clamor, stone by stone,
And streets grow deep with silence, cool and vast,
Where every window holds a lamp alone,
A ghost of daylight that has overpast.
The traffic’s roar has dwindled to a sigh,
A distant siren’s thin, forgotten thread;
The neon signs blink out against the sky,
And all the hurried multitudes are dead.
But I—I am the pulse that will not sleep,
The sharpened breath that cuts the hollow air;
I am the vigil that the shadows keep,
The one who names the darkness, and the dare.
So let the sleepers dream their borrowed peace—
I’ll walk this night until the morning’s lease.
