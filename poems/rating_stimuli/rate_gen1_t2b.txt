The streetlamp hums a thin electric psalm
To empty sidewalks glossed with recent rain.
My footsteps mark the pavement, cold and calm,
A lonely meter tapped in minor strain.
The windows overhead are shutters drawn,
Each yellow square a story locked inside,
While I, a transient in the neon dawn,
Trace cables where the sleeping city’s tied.
But then the wind, a sudden, hungry gust,
Tears at the awning’s seam with ragged nails,
And from the gutter’s dark, a scatter-rust
Whirls up to mock the peace that daylight veils.
So let the fragile glass of quiet break—
I’ll walk this shard-strewn night for my own sake.
