The well of years is deep, and I descend
By crumbling steps where moss and silence meet,
To find the waters that my youth would spend,
A quiet current, cold against the feet.
Each ripple is a face I used to know,
A voice that bends the light of what has been,
And in the glass, the shadows come and go—
The rain that fell, the river's unseen skin.
I cup my hands to hold what will not stay,
The liquid moment slipping through my grip,
And what I drink is only yesterday,
A taste of salt upon the waiting lip.
So let the stream go on, and let it be—
The memory of water stays in me.
