The orchard’s last, bruised apple drops its hold,
And creaking branches bare their knuckled bone;
The wind, a keeper now grown gray and cold,
Unlocks the gate where summer’s warm light shone.
I watch the amber leaves, a scattered fleet,
Set sail upon the gutter’s shallow tide,
And mourn the crisp, bright mornings we would meet
When dew hung heavy on the hillside’s pride.
But in this falling, find a quieter grace—
The slow, deliberate closing of a door,
The settling of dust in a sunlit space,
The harvest’s weight laid down upon the floor.
So let the season’s rigor have its way;
I’ll keep the ember’s glow for darker day.
