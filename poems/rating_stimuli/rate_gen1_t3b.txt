The current, braided bright, unspools the glass
Where every sunken face I knew is kept;
I drag the river’s hem, the silted mass
Of hours that the living currents swept.
A stone I touched when autumn fouled the weir
Still hums against my palm its cold refrain—
The pressure of a thumbprint, sharp and clear,
That fades if I should lift my hand again.
Then sudden stormlight cracks the surface wide:
The wreck of that old bridge, its stanchions torn,
Breaks upward through the flood, and I must ride
The spume that keeps a name I had forsworn.
So let the deep forget what I have held—
One gasp of air, and all the waves are quelled.
