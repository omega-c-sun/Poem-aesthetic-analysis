The maple sheds its coins upon the walk,
A copper flood that clatters at my heel;
The branches twist in skeletal, slow talk,
And strip the final gold they used to feel.
The wind, a workman with a greedy blade,
Shears off the clinging vestiges of grace,
And every tumbling leaf becomes a shade
That cannot find its summer resting place.
But look! The orchard, stripped of all pretense,
Reveals the iron lattice of its boughs,
A stark and sudden architecture, dense
With promise that the frozen earth allows.
So let the season’s ruin be complete;
What falls to ground must rise on stronger feet.
