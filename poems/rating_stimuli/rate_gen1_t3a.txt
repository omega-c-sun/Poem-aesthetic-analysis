The river keeps what daylight cannot hold,
A sunken bell of hours, a coin of years,
And in its murk, a story half-told,
That drifts on currents, deaf to falling tears.
I wade where the shallows ripple, and sift
The silt for a face that the flood erased,
My fingers net a glint, a patient gift—
A shard of mirror where a smile was placed.
But the water tightens, a cold, closing fist,
And the glass slips back to the breathing mud,
Where the shape that I chased no longer exists,
Only the ache of a remembered flood.
So I walk the bank, and the current goes,
While the river keeps what my heart bestows.
