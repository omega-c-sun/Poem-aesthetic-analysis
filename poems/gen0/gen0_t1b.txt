The amber leaves, reluctant, quit the bough,
And drift in spirals on the dying air;
The sun, a ghost, grows paler even now,
And shadows stretch like fingers of despair.
The robin’s song, once rich with summer’s fire,
Is thinned to whispers in the bramble’s gray;
The fields that blazed with gold and green retire
To stubble where the lonely ploughshare may.
But hark! Not all is silence—far above,
A wedge of wild geese cleaves the ashen sky,
Their cries a brazen testament of love
That will not let the fading season die.
So though the world undresses into night,
Their southward flight becomes my stubborn light.
