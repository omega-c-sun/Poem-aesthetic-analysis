The river keeps what daylight cannot hold—
A glint of oar, a whisper on the stone—
And in its deep, where quiet currents fold,
I find the face I thought I had outgrown.
The rain that fell on some forgotten June
Still taps its code along the window ledge,
A pulse that slows beneath a listless moon,
A name I trace along the water’s edge.
What drifts is not the loss but what remains—
The cool, wet gravity of that first glance,
The way a ripple loosens all my chains,
And lets the drowned light surface, slow, by chance.
So let the stream unspool its silver thread—
I drink the past, and it has gone to bed.
