The city sheds its clamor, street by street,
And leaves a hush where engines used to churn.
The pavement holds the memory of feet,
While lonely window-lights begin to burn.
A single figure, cloaked in amber glow,
Moves past the shuttered shops and silent cars,
Whose metal bodies, parked in even row,
Reflect the faded glory of the stars.
The air is thin, a breath of stone and steel,
That cools the fevered pulse of hurried days.
No voice returns the thoughts that turn and wheel,
Lost in the labyrinth of empty ways.
So solitude becomes a quiet friend,
Who walks beside me to the journey’s end.
