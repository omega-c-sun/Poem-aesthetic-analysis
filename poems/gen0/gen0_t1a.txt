The year’s last roses lean to touch the pane,
And swallows stitch the dusk in fading flight;
The orchard’s gold is thinned by coming rain,
And hills are folded in the failing light.
A shutter taps where vines have overgrown,
The garden bench is cold, the paths are bare;
The asters bow their heads on stalks alone,
And silence settles in the waiting air.
No more the urgent hum of summer’s trade,
No more the long, warm hours that used to be;
The season’s promise slowly is unmade,
And what was green is now a memory.
So let the leaves fall quiet, one by one—
The year departs, and so does all begun.
