The river keeps what daylight cannot hold,
a sunken coin, a leaf, a rusted key;
its current turns the story green and cold,
and every ripple drags a thought from me.
I wade through half-remembered autumns, deep
where silt has softed the edges of a name;
my fingers sift the shallows, try to keep
a fragment that the water bends to flame.
But night descends, and all the river swells—
it lunges at the bank with sudden tooth,
and what I thought was mine, the current quells,
a drowning echo severed from its truth.
So let it take the wreckage of my years;
I'll drink the dark and call the flood my tears.
