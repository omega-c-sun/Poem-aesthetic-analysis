The city’s pulse is muffled by the rain,
A silver hush that slicks the iron rails.
My window holds the ghost of a lit train,
Whose fading roar through dripping glass prevails.
The asphalt breathes a spectrum of dull gold,
And gutters choke with the confetti of the hours.
I trace a cold mouth on the pane—a fold
Of fog, a whispered script the lamplight scours.
The clock across the square is stopped at one,
A blind man’s face that cannot see the chill.
The cables overhead, a slackened gun,
Sag with the weight of voices gone to still.
So let the dark unspool its thread of gray;
I wear this solitude like a frayed coat today.
