The city’s iron ribs exhale a breath
Of diesel fumes and rain-washed asphalt gray.
A single lamp-post stitches light from death,
And tracks my shadow’s slow and stolen way.
The pavement drinks the echo of my tread,
A hollow drumbeat on a stage of stone.
The windows, shutters drawn like lids of lead,
Keep vigil for the sleep they must atone.
But here the silence sharpens to a blade—
It slices through the murmur of the wire,
And every distant siren, unafraid,
Becomes a ghost that walks through my desire.
Then, sudden, from the gutter’s choked-up throat,
A star—unbroken—floats upon the moat.
