The maple thrusts its scarlet lance on high,
And wrestles with the wind’s insistent pull,
Whose cold breath strips the gold leaf from the sky
To scatter coins across the river’s hull.
The orchard boughs, once heavy, now unload
Their burden to the furrow’s patient bed,
Where wasps in drunken circles stab and goad
The bruises of the fruit that they have fed.
But see—the swallow’s keel cuts through the gray,
A final stitch that binds the fading hem,
Then snaps the thread and tears the light away,
And leaves the naked scaffolding to stem
The long descent—till winter’s hammer knocks
The last bright season flat against the rocks.
