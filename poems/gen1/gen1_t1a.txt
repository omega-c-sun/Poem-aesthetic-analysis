The orchard yields its last and blunted gold,
And branches, stripped, hold nothing to the sky.
The gathered wind grows sharp, and over-bold,
To teach the lingering leaves the art to fly.
I watch the laden wagon creak and strain
Through ruts of mud, toward the granary door,
While in my hand a single grain of grain
Remembers all the summer’s heavy store.
The light withdraws, a curtain drawn on glass,
And every shadow stretches long and thin,
As if the day, too, knew it could not pass
Unnoticed, and would leave its marks on skin.
So let the season’s turning have its way;
I keep the quiet ache of this last day.
