The ledger of the lake keeps every stone
That ever sank, in silted, patient rows;
The current’s cursive, cursive and alone,
Erases what the surface can’t disclose.
I plunge my hand to sift the muddy cold,
And bring up shards of a forgotten noon—
A sunken coin, a story briefly told,
That darkens as it dries beneath the moon.
The water’s weight is memory’s own press,
A slow compaction of the vanished days,
And what I lift is only loneliness,
A cold, unpolished pebble’s muted grays.
So let the deep keep what the deep has kept;
I’ll turn and leave the ripples, unaccept.
